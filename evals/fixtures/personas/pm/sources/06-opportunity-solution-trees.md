source_type: article

Learning notes. An opportunity solution tree starts with one desired outcome at the top, maps customer opportunities (needs, pains, desires) beneath it, then solutions beneath each opportunity, then small experiments beneath each solution.

Why I like it: it stops teams jumping from an outcome straight to a solution, and it makes it obvious when we have five solutions for one opportunity and none for the rest.

Where it struggles: it can turn into a wall of sticky notes that nobody maintains. I keep ours to one outcome per quarter.
